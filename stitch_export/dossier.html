01 / OVERVIEW • Analytical Framework

Executive Literature Review & Synthesis Framework

Climate risk modeling for the South Asian subcontinent has historically suffered from systemic fragmentation: atmospheric dynamics models operate at low resolution (25–50 km²), while vulnerability frameworks deployed by humanitarian agencies operate qualitatively at district or sub-district levels without synchronizing real-time satellite telemetry.

02 / MATHEMATICAL ARCHITECTURE • Composite Risk Index

Algorithmic Formulation of Compound Vulnerability

Standardized under the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction and adapted for multi-temporal satellite feeds, the climate risk coefficient ($\mathcal{R}_{it}$) for geographic cell $i$ at time interval $t$ is formulated as an exponential interaction tensor:

Governing Formulation
Risk_{i,t} = \left[ \frac{\mathcal{H}_{i,t} \times \mathcal{E}_{i} \times \mathcal{S}_{i}}{\mathcal{A}_{i}} \right]^{\gamma} \cdot \prod_{k=1}^{n} (1 + \omega_k \cdot \Delta_{k,i,t})

Where $\mathcal{H}$ represents Hazard Magnitude, $\mathcal{E}$ is Asset Exposure, $\mathcal{S}$ is Physical Sensitivity, and $\mathcal{A}$ is Localized Adaptive Capacity. $\omega_k$ measures non-linear tipping-point thresholds.
